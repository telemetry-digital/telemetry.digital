> **Methodological note for the report writer — read first.**
> The official OJ text of 2013/C 343/01 is served by EUR-Lex as a PDF/HTML that the fetch tooling

## Q1. What does 2013/C 343/01 say exactly about mapping, qualification/calibration, monitoring, alarms, deviations and retention — with section numbers?

### Takeaway
The guideline is structured by sub-section (3.1 Principle, 3.2 Premises, **3.2.1 Temperature and environment control**, 3.3 Equipment, **3.3.1 Computerised systems**, 3.3.2 Qualification and validation), not by continuously numbered paragraphs. Its temperature-monitoring content is remarkably short — a handful of sentences — and is written almost entirely in "should" language, which under EU practice makes it *expected* rather than legally prescriptive, while the underlying obligation is the legally binding Article 80(d)/84 of Directive 2001/83/EC.

### Cited Findings

**Structure of Chapter 3 confirmed as sub-sectional (3.1 / 3.2 / 3.2.1 / 3.3 / 3.3.1 / 3.3.2)** — [EUR-Lex, OJ C 343/1](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)

**3.1. Principle** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "Wholesale distributors must have suitable and adequate premises, installations and equipment, so as to ensure proper storage and distribution of medicinal products."

Note the use of **"must"** here — this sentence mirrors the statutory wording of Article 79(b)/80 of Directive 2001/83/EC and is one of the few genuinely mandatory formulations in Chapter 3. — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)

**3.2. Premises** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "The premises should be designed or adapted to ensure that the required storage conditions are maintained."

> "Medicinal products should be stored in segregated areas which are clearly marked and have access restricted to authorised personnel."

> "Any falsified medicinal products, expired products, recalled products and rejected products found in the supply chain should be immediately physically segregated."

**3.2.1. Temperature and environment control** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "Suitable equipment and procedures should be in place to check the environment where medicinal products are stored."

> "An initial temperature mapping exercise should be carried out on the storage area before use, under representative conditions."

The same first sentence of the mapping requirement is quoted independently by the MHRA Inspectorate blog, which attributes it to GDP 3.2.1 — [MHRA Inspectorate, "Temperature mapping – an introduction"](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)

**3.3. Equipment** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "All equipment impacting on storage and distribution of medicinal products should be designed, located and maintained to a standard which suits its intended purpose."

> "Equipment used to control or to monitor the environment where the medicinal products are stored should be calibrated at defined intervals."

**3.3.1. Computerised systems** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "Before a computerised system is brought into use, it should be demonstrated that the system is capable of achieving the desired results accurately, consistently and reproducibly."

**3.3.2. Qualification and validation** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "Wholesale distributors should identify what key equipment qualification and/or key process validation is necessary to ensure correct installation and operation."

**Chapter 4 — Documentation, section 4.2 (General)** — [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]
> "Good documentation constitutes an essential part of the quality system."

> "Documentation should be sufficiently comprehensive with respect to the scope of the wholesale distributor's activities."

**Retention period — 5 years, but anchored to national law.** An independently fetched European Commission copy of the GDP guidelines renders the retention rule as: *"Documents should be retained for a period stated in national legislation but not shorter than 5 years."* and applies the same formula to computerised-system data: data must be protected *"for a period stated in national legislation but at least 5 years."* — [European Commission, Guidelines on Good Distribution Practice of Medicinal Products for Human Use (PDF)](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)

**Calibration traceability and alarms.** The same Commission copy renders the equipment rules as:
> "Equipment used to control or to monitor the environment of the medicinal product, should be calibrated and their correct operation and suitability for purpose verified at defined intervals by the appropriate methodology."

> "Calibration of equipment should be traceable to a primary standard."

> "Appropriate alarm systems should be in place to provide alerts when there are excursions [deviations] from pre-defined storage conditions."

> "Adequate records of repair, maintenance and calibration activities for key equipment should be made and the results should be retained."

> (Computerised systems) "Before a computerised system is brought into use, it should be confirmed as being capable of achieving the desired results including full traceability of distribution operations."

> (Qualification and validation) "Wholesale distributors should identify what qualification and/or validation work is necessary to demonstrate control of key aspects of their activities."

> "Storage areas should be temperature mapped under representative conditions and should take into account seasonal variations."

— all from [European Commission GDP guidelines PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)

### ⚠️ Source conflict that must be flagged in the report
The Commission-hosted PDF above was transcribed by the retrieval tool with **continuous paragraph numbering** (Principle 3.0; then 3.13 environment control, 3.14 mapping, 3.15 equipment design, 3.16 calibration/verification, 3.17–3.18 traceability to a primary standard + alarm systems, 3.19 records of repair/maintenance/calibration, 3.20+ computerised systems, 3.26+ qualification and validation, 4.5 retention). The **official OJ C 343/01 text does not use that numbering** — it uses 3.1 / 3.2 / 3.2.1 / 3.3 / 3.3.1 / 3.3.2 with unnumbered paragraphs inside each sub-section. Two explanations are possible and I could not distinguish them with the access available:
1. the Commission PDF is a re-laid-out/consolidated edition that added paragraph numbers, or
2. the retrieval tool's summariser generated the paragraph numbers.

**Recommendation: in the final report cite the sub-section numbers (3.2.1, 3.3, 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 4.2) — those are confirmed against EUR-Lex — and do NOT cite "3.13/3.14/3.16/3.18" as OJ paragraph references without verification.** The *wording* of the sentences above is consistent between both sources and is safe to quote; only the numbering is in doubt.

### Inferences
- Chapter 3's temperature content is deliberately outcome-based: it states *that* mapping, calibration, traceability, alarms and records are required, and delegates *how* entirely to the distributor's risk assessment. For a vendor of monitoring hardware/software this means there is no EU-level technical specification to "certify" against — the compliance argument has to be built per installation, in the qualification documentation.
- The retention rule ("national legislation, but not shorter than 5 years") means temperature data produced by a monitoring platform is a GDP record with a minimum 5-year retention obligation — this is a hard product requirement for data architecture (export, immutability, readability over 5+ years), not an optional feature.

### Gaps
- The full, uninterrupted paragraph text of 3.2.1 could not be retrieved. In particular the sentences that (from the structure of the guideline) follow the "initial temperature mapping" sentence — concerning **placement of monitoring equipment according to the results of the mapping exercise**, and **repetition of mapping following risk assessment or significant modification of the facility or the temperature-controlling equipment** — are strongly indicated by the MHRA blog's paraphrase but **I could not obtain them as confirmed verbatim OJ text**. The writer should pull these two sentences directly from the OJ PDF.
- Chapter 5 (Operations) and Chapter 9 (Transportation) temperature provisions were outside the retrieved extracts.
- The **Slovak-language version** of 2013/C 343/01 was not retrieved (EUR-Lex was not reachable for a second language version within the tool budget).

## Q2. Is 2013/C 343/01 still the current text as of September 2026?

### Takeaway
Yes. No revision of the human-medicines GDP guidelines had replaced 2013/C 343/01 as of September 2026. A 2026 Commission document that surfaces in searches for "GDP guidelines 2026" is a **false positive** — it is a GMP annex, not GDP.

### Cited Findings
- EMA's Good Distribution Practice page identifies the applicable guideline as **"Guidelines on GDP of medicinal products for human use" (2013/C 343/01), published 23 November 2013**, and **does not mention any scheduled 2026 revision**. — [EMA, Good distribution practice](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/compliance-post-authorisation/good-distribution-practice)
- The document **C(2026) 4135 final, Brussels, 23.6.2026**, which appears in search results alongside GDP queries, is in fact **"EU Guidelines for Good Manufacturing Practice for Medicinal Products for Human and Veterinary Use, Annex 19: Reference and Retention Samples"**, a revision of the 2006 version of Annex 19, with legal basis Article 47 of Directive 2001/83/EC and Commission Directive (EU) 2017/1572. It applies **3 months from publication** by the Commission. It has nothing to do with distribution. — [European Commission C(2026) 4135 final](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/831d392e-bcc4-4552-8011-2b724b8655de_en?filename=c2026-4135_en_act_en.pdf)
- EMA also notes that the COVID-era temporary extensions of GDP certificate validity (granted to end-2024) **"no longer applies"** from 2025; extensions are now decided by Member States case by case. GDP certificates and non-compliance statements are entered into **EudraGMDP**, described as *"a publicly accessible EU database containing all wholesale distribution authorisations and details of registered importers and distributors of active substances in the EEA."* — [EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/compliance-post-authorisation/good-distribution-practice)

### Inferences
- Any marketing claim of "compliant with the latest 2026 EU GDP" would be wrong; the correct anchor remains the 2013 guideline. This is worth telling the user explicitly, since search engines will keep surfacing the Annex 19 document.

### Gaps
- I found reporting of a broader "EU pharma package 2026" affecting GMP/GDP operators, but could not verify from a primary Commission source whether a GDP guideline revision is formally in the legislative pipeline. Not asserted here.

## Q3. Relationship to Article 84 and Article 85b(3) of Directive 2001/83/EC

### Takeaway
The GDP guidelines are non-legislative Commission guidelines adopted *under* Directive 2001/83/EC: Article 84 is the empowerment to publish GDP guidelines for medicinal products, and Article 85b(3) extends the same guidelines to brokers of medicinal products.

### Cited Findings
- EMA's GDP page confirms the guidelines implement requirements from **Directive 2001/83/EC, with Articles 84 and 85b(3) referenced in the legal framework**, though the page does not reproduce their text. — [EMA, Good distribution practice](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/compliance-post-authorisation/good-distribution-practice)
- The title of the act itself is **"Guidelines of 5 November 2013 on Good Distribution Practice of medicinal products for human use"**, CELEX **52013XC1123(01)**, published in **OJ C 343, 23.11.2013**. — [EUR-Lex CELEX 52013XC1123(01)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52013XC1123(01))

### Inferences
- Because the instrument is a **Commission "Guideline" published in the C series** (communications/information), not in the L series (legislation), it is not directly binding in the way a regulation is. Its practical force comes from (a) Article 80(d) of Directive 2001/83/EC, which obliges wholesale distribution authorisation holders to comply with GDP principles, and (b) national transposition — in Slovakia, the Medicines Act (zákon č. 362/2011 Z. z.) and ŠÚKL inspection practice. **This distinction — "requirement" vs "guideline that inspectors enforce" — is exactly the nuance the user asked to have marked.**

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the verbatim text of Article 84 or Article 85b(3) of Directive 2001/83/EC, nor the recital of 2013/C 343/01 that cites them. The writer should quote these from the consolidated Directive on EUR-Lex.
- I did not verify the Slovak national retention period (whether Slovak law sets a period longer than the 5-year GDP floor). **This should be checked against zákon č. 362/2011 Z. z. and any ŠÚKL guidance before the user relies on "5 years" for a Slovak customer.**

## Q4. Temperature mapping methodology — sensor count, duration, seasonal mapping, worst-case locations, re-mapping

### Takeaway
EU GDP itself prescribes **no numbers at all** — only "initial mapping before use, under representative conditions", placement of monitors per the mapping result, and repetition on risk assessment / significant modification. Every concrete figure (5–10 m grid, 24–72 h, 7 days, 3-yearly) comes from **WHO technical supplements**, which are recommendations, not EU requirements — but which EU inspectors, including MHRA, treat as the reference methodology.

### Cited Findings — WHO (the source of all the concrete numbers)
From **WHO Technical Report Series 961, Annex 9, Supplement 8 — "Temperature mapping of storage areas"**:
- **Sensor grid:** *"EDLMs should be arranged in a grid fashion along the width and length of the area so that the area is reasonably covered, with EDLMs located every 5–10 metres."* (Section 2.2.6, Step 5) — [WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 Supplement 8](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Vertical placement:** *"If the ceiling height is 3.6 metres or less, position EDLMs directly above one another at high, medium and low level"*; above 3.6 m, position at bottom, middle and top. (Section 2.2.6, Step 5) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Duration, temperature-controlled storage:** *"The mapping study should be run for between 24 and 72 hours, or longer if justified"* (Section 2.2.6, Step 9) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Duration, ambient/warehouse storage:** should be run for *"a minimum of seven consecutive days – including five working days and two weekend days"* (Section 2.2.6, Step 9) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Seasonal mapping:** *"At least two temperature-mapping studies may be needed in each area to observe the effect of seasonal variation"* — one in the warmest season, one in the coldest. (Section 2.2.4) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Hot/cold spots drive monitor placement:** hot and cold spot determination identifies *"the locations where the monitoring system sensors should preferentially be located."* (Section 2.4.2) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Re-mapping:** periodic mapping *"every three years"* may be required; re-mapping is additionally triggered by *"significant modifications"*, unexplained variability in routine monitoring, and assessments following power failure. (Section 1.1) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- **Mapping sensor accuracy:** devices should have *"a US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)-traceable 3-point calibration"* with *"an error of no more than ± 0.5 °C at each calibration point."* (Section 2.1) — [same](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)

Related WHO documents in the same family (identified, not fully extracted):
- **WHO TRS 961, Annex 9, Supplement 7 — "Qualification of temperature-controlled storage areas"** — [WHO](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp7.pdf)
- **WHO TRS 992, Annex 5 — "Technical supplements to Model guidance for the storage and transport of time- and temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical products"**, which consolidates the TRS 961 Annex 9 supplements — [WHO TRS 992 Annex 5](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/trs992-annex5) / [PDF](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs992-annex5.pdf?sfvrsn=99aedfbe_2)
- WHO practical guide **"How to temperature map cold chain equipment and storage areas"** (2022) — [PDF](https://www.coldchainplatform.com/resources/pdf/en/How-to-temperature-map-cold-chain-equipment-and-storage-areas_WHO_20220411.pdf)

### Cited Findings — MHRA (UK inspectorate, practical interpretation)
- MHRA quotes the GDP requirement as *"An initial temperature mapping exercise should be carried out on the storage area before use, under representative conditions."* and stresses that monitoring equipment placement should reflect the mapping results, identifying areas with temperature extremes. — [MHRA Inspectorate blog](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- MHRA does **not** set a sensor count: it requires *"calibrated monitoring probes in sufficient numbers dependant on the size of the storage area"*, with no fixed quantity. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- Permanent monitors should be *"positioned in the areas that experience the extremes of fluctuations"* and cover *"hot or cold spots"* identified during mapping. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- Mapping *"should be repeated according to the results of a risk assessment exercise"* and specifically to *"cover seasonal variations"*; winter and summer studies capture different influences (heating vs solar gain). Re-mapping triggers include significant facility or temperature-control equipment modification, and reconfiguration of storage areas. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- On duration, MHRA does **not** mandate a period; the blog reports the common industry approach as roughly **one week**, to capture workflow variation and daily temperature cycles. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- MHRA describes annual calibration of equipment, with control thermometers checked yearly against calibrated reference standards. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- MHRA emphasises that a **documented risk assessment must justify** mapping frequency and probe placement, and define the ongoing monitoring regime — typically reviewed during self-inspection. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)

### Cited Findings — industry practice summary (explicitly labelled as such)
A commercial GDP-mapping guide separates the two tiers as follows, which is a useful framing:
- **Treated as requirements:** mapping before first use; documented risk assessment justifying sensor placement; Responsible Person sign-off on protocol and results; a documented link between mapping results and permanent monitoring sensor placement; calibration traceability to ISO/IEC 17025 standards.
- **Treated as recommendations:** 48–72 h duration; the WHO 5–10 m grid spacing as a starting point; challenge tests (door-opening, controlled power interruption); seasonal studies in ambient warehouses.
- It states there is **"no mandatory re-mapping interval"** in EU GDP, and that re-mapping is triggered by facility/layout changes, HVAC or control modification, operational shifts (throughput, new shifts, door activity), adverse monitoring trends (alarm clusters, rising variability), and seasonal extremes.
- It cites a **48-hour minimum** for steady-state runs, longer for complex HVAC.
— [Eupry, GDP temperature mapping guide](https://eupry.com/gdp/gdp-mapping/) *(vendor source — use as indicative of industry practice, not as authority)*

### Inferences
- **Requirement vs recommendation, stated plainly for the user:** *mapping itself* is a GDP expectation (3.2.1); *every number* — 5–10 m, 24–72 h, 7 days, 3-yearly, ±0.5 °C — is WHO recommendation or industry practice. A Slovak installer should therefore write the numbers into the customer's **mapping protocol and risk assessment**, citing WHO as the justification, rather than claiming EU GDP mandates them.
- The strongest commercial hook for a monitoring-software vendor is the chain **mapping → hot/cold spots → fixed sensor positions → alarm limits**. Both WHO 2.4.2 and MHRA state monitors must sit at the extremes found by mapping. Software that stores the mapping report and ties each fixed sensor to its justified position closes an audit finding that inspectors routinely raise.
- WHO's "every three years" is the only periodic re-mapping figure found in any of the sources; EU GDP and MHRA both decline to set one and defer to risk assessment.

### Gaps
- No source gives a **sensors-per-cubic-metre or per-pallet-position formula**. The 5–10 m grid is the only density rule found, and WHO frames it as "reasonably covered", not as a calculation.
- I did not obtain the **PIC/S PE 011 GDP Guide** for medicinal products. The PIC/S document retrieved at `picscheme.org/docview/2466` turned out to be **PI 047-1, "Guidelines on the Principles of Good Distribution Practice of Active Substances for Medicinal Products for Human Use", 1 July 2018** — i.e. the *active substances* guide, not PE 011. Its relevant provisions are noted below for completeness, but **it does not answer the medicinal-products GDP question.**
- WHO TRS 992 Annex 2 (Model guidance for storage and transport) was not separately extracted; Annex 5 of TRS 992 was identified as the consolidated technical supplements.

## Q5. Calibration of monitoring equipment — traceability, frequency, accredited laboratories, measurement uncertainty

### Takeaway
GDP requires calibration at defined intervals, traceable to a primary standard, with records retained — but names no interval, no accreditation requirement and no uncertainty budget. Annual calibration and ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratories are inspectorate expectation and industry norm rather than guideline text.

### Cited Findings
- GDP 3.3 [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]: *"Equipment used to control or to monitor the environment where the medicinal products are stored should be calibrated at defined intervals."* — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)
- Extended Commission-copy rendering: *"Equipment used to control or to monitor the environment of the medicinal product, should be calibrated and their correct operation and suitability for purpose verified at defined intervals by the appropriate methodology."*; *"Calibration of equipment should be traceable to a primary standard."*; *"Adequate records of repair, maintenance and calibration activities for key equipment should be made and the results should be retained."* — [European Commission GDP PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)
- MHRA practice: equipment calibrated **annually**, with control thermometers checked yearly against calibrated reference standards. — [MHRA Inspectorate](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/)
- WHO, for *mapping* loggers specifically: NIST-traceable **3-point calibration** with error **no more than ±0.5 °C at each calibration point**. — [WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 Supp 8, §2.1](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- Industry framing treats **calibration traceability to ISO/IEC 17025** as a requirement in practice. — [Eupry](https://eupry.com/gdp/gdp-mapping/) *(vendor source)*
- For comparison, PIC/S **PI 047-1** (active substances, 1 July 2018), §5.3: *"Monitoring devices that are necessary to guarantee the quality attributes of the active substance should be calibrated according to an approved schedule against certified traceable standards."* — [PIC/S PI 047-1](https://picscheme.org/docview/2466)

### Inferences
- "Traceable to a primary standard" is the operative phrase for a hardware vendor: each LoRaWAN sensor shipped into a GDP site needs a calibration certificate with an unbroken traceability chain to a national/international standard. ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation of the issuing laboratory is the ordinary way to demonstrate that, but GDP does not name it.
- GDP's silence on **measurement uncertainty** is notable. Nothing in the retrieved EU text requires an uncertainty statement. WHO's ±0.5 °C is the nearest thing to a numerical accuracy expectation, and it is scoped to mapping loggers, not to permanently installed monitors. A vendor claiming "GDP-compliant accuracy" is making a claim GDP does not define.
- "Verified at defined intervals" (Commission rendering) implies GDP contemplates **two distinct activities**: periodic laboratory calibration, and in-service verification/checks. Software that logs both is aligned with the text.

### Gaps
- No EU source found that specifies a calibration **interval** (the annual figure is MHRA practice, not guideline text).
- No EU GDP requirement found for the calibration laboratory to be **accredited**; I found no primary source stating this, only industry assertion.
- No EU GDP treatment of **measurement uncertainty** or of how uncertainty should be applied against alarm limits (e.g. whether a 2–8 °C limit should be tightened by the sensor's uncertainty). This is a genuine gap in the guideline, not just in my research.

## Q6. Alarm systems, deviation management, out-of-hours and power failure

### Takeaway
GDP contains a single short sentence on alarms and no operational detail whatsoever — no alarm limits, no testing frequency, no escalation rules, no out-of-hours requirement, no power-failure provision. This is the thinnest area of the guideline and the area where a monitoring-software vendor has the most latitude and the most need to document its own rationale.

### Cited Findings
- The only alarm provision located: *"Appropriate alarm systems should be in place to provide alerts when there are excursions [deviations] from pre-defined storage conditions."* — [European Commission GDP PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)
- WHO links **power failure** to re-mapping: assessments following a power failure are among the triggers for re-mapping a storage area. — [WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 Supp 8, §1.1](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/distribution/trs961-annex9-supp8.pdf)
- WHO/industry treat **controlled power-interruption studies** and door-opening simulations as *recommended additions* to mapping, not requirements. — [Eupry](https://eupry.com/gdp/gdp-mapping/) *(vendor source)*
- MHRA links alarm behaviour back to monitoring design: adverse monitoring trends such as **alarm clusters and rising variability** are grounds for re-mapping. — [MHRA](https://mhrainspectorate.blog.gov.uk/2016/07/14/temperature-mapping-an-introduction/); [Eupry](https://eupry.com/gdp/gdp-mapping/)

### Inferences
- Because GDP says only "appropriate", the burden falls entirely on the site's quality system to define what "appropriate" means: alarm set-points relative to the licensed storage condition, delay/dwell times, who is notified, escalation if unacknowledged, and coverage at nights/weekends. **A monitoring platform that makes these configurable and auditable is selling exactly the evidence the inspector will ask for.**
- Alarm *testing* frequency is not mentioned anywhere in the retrieved EU text. It is, however, implied by GDP 3.3's "correct operation and suitability for purpose verified at defined intervals", which on a plain reading covers the alarm chain as part of the monitoring equipment.
- Deviation management is handled in GDP Chapter 1 (Quality management) through complaints/deviations/CAPA and change control rather than in Chapter 3 — a temperature excursion is a deviation to be investigated and the affected stock assessed before release. I could not retrieve Chapter 1 text to confirm the exact wording.

### Gaps
- **No verbatim GDP text on deviation management / CAPA** was retrieved (Chapter 1). The writer should pull GDP 1.x on deviations, change control, quality risk management (GDP 1.5) and management review from the OJ text.
- **No source found** in EU GDP specifying alarm testing frequency, escalation chains, out-of-hours coverage, or back-up power. These appear to be genuinely absent from the guideline and governed only by the distributor's own procedures and the Responsible Person's judgement. This absence should be stated as a finding, not filled with invented requirements.
- GDP Chapter 5 (Operations) provisions on handling of temperature excursions and stock disposition were not retrieved.

## Q7. Computerised systems and the relationship to GMP Annex 11

### Takeaway
GDP 3.3.1 requires computerised systems to be demonstrated fit for purpose before use, with data protection for at least the national retention period (minimum 5 years). The retrieved sources did **not** confirm an explicit cross-reference from GDP to GMP Annex 11.

### Cited Findings
- GDP 3.3.1 [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]: *"Before a computerised system is brought into use, it should be demonstrated that the system is capable of achieving the desired results accurately, consistently and reproducibly."* — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)
- Commission-copy rendering adds traceability: *"Before a computerised system is brought into use, it should be confirmed as being capable of achieving the desired results including full traceability of distribution operations."*, and requires data protection *"for a period stated in national legislation but at least 5 years."* — [European Commission GDP PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)
- GDP 3.3.2 [VERBATIM — EUR-Lex]: *"Wholesale distributors should identify what key equipment qualification and/or key process validation is necessary to ensure correct installation and operation."* — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)
- Commission-copy rendering of the qualification clause: *"Wholesale distributors should identify what qualification and/or validation work is necessary to demonstrate control of key aspects of their activities."*, with sections requiring documented risk assessments and validation reports with acceptance criteria. — [European Commission GDP PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/9f28179a-a6f8-418a-a5fc-ecfbb3ae3ba8_en)

### Inferences
- The practical qualification model for a temperature-monitoring installation follows straight from 3.3.2 + 3.3.1: a risk assessment defining "key", then URS → IQ → OQ → PQ with pre-defined acceptance criteria and a validation report, plus system-level evidence of accuracy/consistency/reproducibility and full traceability (audit trail, user access control, data integrity). This is what a vendor like telemetry.digital would supply as a qualification pack.
- GDP's requirement that data be protected for the retention period means the monitoring software — not just the site's paper records — is inside the GDP record-retention scope. Backup, restore testing and long-term readability are compliance features, not nice-to-haves.

### Gaps

It is plausible that Annex 11 is applied to GDP computerised systems by *inspectorate expectation and analogy* rather than by an explicit textual cross-reference in the GDP guideline. **This should be verified against the OJ text before the report asserts a cross-reference.** Do not state that GDP Chapter 3.3 cites Annex 11 unless the OJ text is checked.

## Q8. Pharmacies (as distinct from wholesale warehouses)

### Takeaway
2013/C 343/01 addresses **wholesale distributors** (and, via Article 85b(3), brokers). It does not regulate retail/community pharmacy storage as such; pharmacy-level temperature control in Slovakia derives from national pharmacy legislation and from the product's authorised storage conditions, not from this guideline.

### Cited Findings
- Every operative provision retrieved is addressed to "wholesale distributors" — e.g. GDP 3.1: *"Wholesale distributors must have suitable and adequate premises..."*; 3.3.2: *"Wholesale distributors should identify what key equipment qualification..."* — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:C:2013:343:0001:0014:EN:PDF)
- EMA frames the guidelines as applying to wholesale distribution authorisation holders, with certificates recorded in EudraGMDP alongside wholesale distribution authorisations. — [EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/compliance-post-authorisation/good-distribution-practice)

### Inferences
- For the user's sales positioning this is an important distinction: a **pharmaceutical warehouse / wholesaler** customer is squarely within GDP and will be inspected against it; a **pharmacy** customer is buying the same technology but the regulatory driver is different (national pharmacy rules, SmPC storage conditions, and — for pharmacies that also hold a wholesale licence — GDP). Selling both with identical "GDP compliance" messaging would be imprecise.

### Gaps
- I did not research Slovak national pharmacy storage requirements or ŠÚKL guidance for pharmacies; that is outside the retrieved material and should be treated as a separate research task.
