## Q1: Verbatim clauses of EudraLex Vol. 4 Annex 15 (URS, DQ, IQ, OQ, PQ, Change Control, VMP)

### Takeaway
Annex 15 is short and prescriptive. The qualification chain lives in **Section 3 (clauses 3.1–3.14)**, the VMP in **clauses 1.4–1.5**, documentation rules in **Section 2 (2.1–2.6)**, re-qualification in **Section 4 (4.1–4.2)**, and change control in **Section 11 (11.1–11.5)**. Crucially for a paid validation service, **clause 2.6 explicitly contemplates third-party-supplied validation protocols** but puts the confirmation burden on the customer site.

### Cited Findings — Section 1: Organising and Planning for Qualification and Validation

- **1.1** [VERBATIM] "All qualification and validation activities should be planned and take the life cycle of facilities, equipment, utilities, process and product into consideration." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **1.2** [VERBATIM] "Qualification and validation activities should only be performed by suitably trained personnel who follow approved procedures." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **1.3** [VERBATIM] "Qualification/validation personnel should report as defined in the pharmaceutical quality system although this may not necessarily be to a quality management or a quality assurance function. However, there should be appropriate quality oversight over the whole validation life cycle." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **1.4 (VMP)** [VERBATIM] "The key elements of the site qualification and validation programme should be clearly defined and documented in a validation master plan (VMP) or equivalent document." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **1.5 (VMP contents)** [VERBATIM] "The VMP or equivalent document should define the qualification/validation system and include or reference information on at least the following:
  - i. Qualification and Validation policy
  - ii. The organisational structure including roles and responsibilities for qualification and validation activities
  - iii. Summary of the facilities, equipment, systems, processes on site and the qualification and validation status
  - iv. Change control and deviation management for qualification and validation
  - v. Guidance on developing acceptance criteria
  - vi. References to existing documents
  - vii. The qualification and validation strategy, including requalification, where applicable." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)

### Cited Findings — Section 2: Documentation, including VMP

- **2.1** [VERBATIM] "Good documentation practices are important to support knowledge management throughout the product lifecycle." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **2.2** [VERBATIM] "All documents generated during qualification and validation should be approved and authorised by appropriate personnel as defined in the pharmaceutical quality system." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **2.3** [VERBATIM] "The inter-relationship between documents in complex validation projects should be clearly defined." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **2.4** [VERBATIM] "Validation protocols should be prepared which defines the critical systems, attributes and parameters and the associated acceptance criteria." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **2.5** [VERBATIM] "Qualification documents may be combined together, where appropriate, e.g. installation qualification (IQ) and operational qualification (OQ)." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf) — *(This is the clause that licenses a combined IOQ protocol, common for monitoring systems.)*
- **2.6 (third-party validation services — THE key clause for a paid validation package)** [VERBATIM] "Where validation protocols and other documentation are supplied by a third party providing validation services, appropriate personnel at the manufacturing site should confirm suitability and compliance with internal procedures before approval." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)

### Cited Findings — Section 3: Qualification stages for equipment, facilities, utilities and systems

- **3.1** [VERBATIM] "Qualification activities should consider all stages from initial development of the user requirements specification through to the end of use of the equipment, facility, utility or system." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.2 (URS)** [VERBATIM] "The specification for equipment, facilities, utilities or systems should be defined in a URS and/or a functional specification." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf) — *Note: the full official clause 3.2 continues with wording to the effect that the essential elements of quality need to be built in at this stage and any GMP risks mitigated to an acceptable level; this continuation was not returned verbatim by the fetch and should be verified against the PDF.*
- **3.3 (DQ)** [VERBATIM] "The next element in the qualification of equipment, facilities, utilities, or systems is DQ where the compliance of the design with GMP should be demonstrated and documented." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.4 (FAT)** [VERBATIM] "Equipment, especially if incorporating novel or complex technology, may be evaluated, if applicable, at the vendor prior to delivery." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.5** [VERBATIM] "Prior to installation, equipment should be confirmed to comply with the URS/functional specification at the vendor site, if applicable." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.6 (leveraging FAT — avoids re-testing on site)** [VERBATIM] "Where appropriate and justified, documentation review and some tests could be performed at the FAT or other stages without the need to repeat on site at IQ/OQ if it can be shown that the functionality is not affected by the transport and installation." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.7 (SAT)** [VERBATIM] "FAT may be supplemented by the execution of a SAT following the receipt of equipment at the manufacturing site." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.8 (IQ)** [VERBATIM] "IQ should be performed on equipment, facilities, utilities, or systems." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.9 (IQ contents)** [VERBATIM] "IQ should include, but is not limited to the following:
  - i. Verification of the correct installation of components, instrumentation, equipment, pipe work and services against the engineering drawings and specifications
  - ii. Verification of the correct installation against pre-defined criteria
  - iii. Collection and collation of supplier operating and working instructions and maintenance requirements
  - iv. Calibration of instrumentation
  - v. Verification of the materials of construction." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.10 (IOQ)** [VERBATIM] "OQ normally follows IQ but depending on the complexity of the equipment, it may be performed as a combined Installation/Operation Qualification (IOQ)." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.11 (OQ contents)** [VERBATIM] "OQ should include but is not limited to the following:
  - i. Tests that have been developed from the knowledge of processes, systems and equipment to ensure the system is operating as designed
  - ii. Tests to confirm upper and lower operating limits, and /or 'worst case' conditions." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.12** [VERBATIM] "The completion of a successful OQ should allow the finalisation of standard operating and cleaning procedures, operator training and preventative maintenance requirements." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.13 (PQ)** [VERBATIM] "PQ should normally follow the successful completion of IQ and OQ. However, it may in some cases be appropriate to perform it in conjunction with OQ or Process Validation." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **3.14 (PQ contents)** [VERBATIM] "PQ should include, but is not limited to the following:
  - i. Tests, using production materials, qualified substitutes or simulated product proven to have equivalent behaviour under normal operating conditions with worst case batch sizes.
  - ii. Tests should cover the operating range of the intended process, unless documented evidence from the development phases confirming the operational ranges is available." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)

### Cited Findings — Section 4: Re-qualification

- **4.1** [VERBATIM] "Equipment, facilities, utilities and systems should be evaluated at an appropriate frequency to confirm that they remain in a state of control." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **4.2** [VERBATIM] "Where re-qualification is necessary and performed at a specific time period, the period should be justified and the criteria for evaluation defined." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf) — *Note: the official clause 4.2 also contains wording about the possibility of assessing for small changes over time; verify.*
- Clause **4.3 does not exist** — Section 4 ends at 4.2. — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)

### Cited Findings — Section 11: Change control

- **11.1** [VERBATIM] "The control of change is an important part of knowledge management and should be handled within the pharmaceutical quality system." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **11.2** [VERBATIM] "Written procedures should be in place to describe the actions to be taken if a planned change is proposed to a starting material, product component, process, equipment, premises, product range, method of production or testing, batch size, design space or any other change during the lifecycle that may affect product quality or reproducibility." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **11.3** [VERBATIM] "Where design space is used, the impact on changes to the design space should be considered against the registered design space within the marketing authorisation and the need for any regulatory actions assessed." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **11.4 (the clause that drives regression/requalification after a software release)** [VERBATIM] "Quality risk management should be used to evaluate planned changes to determine the potential impact on product quality, pharmaceutical quality systems, documentation, validation, regulatory status, calibration, maintenance and on any other system to avoid unintended consequences and to plan for any necessary process validation, verification or requalification efforts." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- **11.5** [VERBATIM] "Changes should be authorised and approved by the responsible persons or relevant functional personnel in accordance with the pharmaceutical quality system." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- The exact document set that Annex 15 forces a validation package to contain, read literally: **VMP (or reference to the customer's VMP) → URS and/or Functional Specification (3.2) → DQ (3.3) → optional FAT/SAT (3.4–3.7) → IQ protocol + report (3.8–3.9) → OQ protocol + report (3.10–3.11) → PQ protocol + report (3.13–3.14) → Validation Summary Report → change control and re-qualification strategy (1.5 vii, 4.1–4.2, 11.x)**. Every one of those has a named clause behind it, which is exactly what a paid package should cite on its cover page.
- Clause 2.6 is the commercial hook: a third party (the sole trader) may legitimately author and supply the protocols, but the deliverable must be written so the customer's QA can review and approve it against their own SOPs. Practically this means leaving customer approval signature blocks, a customer document number field, and a statement of compliance with the customer's validation SOP.
- Clause 2.5 justifies selling a combined **IOQ** for small installations, which reduces the price point without weakening compliance.
- Clause 3.6 is the argument for doing a **FAT on the server/software at the supplier's own premises** (configured test instance) and not repeating it all on site — a real cost saver, but it must be documented and justified.

### Gaps
- The continuation sentences of clauses 3.2 and 4.2 were not returned word-for-word. The full official PDF must be consulted for a complete verbatim quote.
- Annex 15 Section 5 (Process Validation), 6 (Verification of transportation), 8 (Qualification of utilities) and the Glossary were not transcribed; Section 6 on transportation verification may be relevant if the customer also ships goods, and should be checked separately.

## Q2: Annex 11 (Computerised Systems) — the clauses that actually govern a monitoring *software* system

### Takeaway
Annex 15 governs qualification generally; **Annex 11 is the annex that specifically governs the software**, and for a LoRaWAN + bespoke-software monitoring system it is the more important of the two. It is the source of the requirements on supplier assessment (3.x), URS (4.4), bespoke-system lifecycle (4.6), testing evidence (4.7), audit trail (9), change management (10), periodic evaluation (11), security (12), backup (7.2) and business continuity (16).

### Cited Findings (all [PARTIAL] — abbreviated by the fetch tool; verify against the official PDF before commercial use)

- **Principle** [PARTIAL] "This annex applies to all forms of computerised systems used as part of a GMP regulated activities." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- **1 Risk Management** [PARTIAL] "Risk management should be applied throughout the lifecycle of the computerised system taking into account patient safety, data integrity and product quality." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- **2 Personnel** [PARTIAL] "All personnel should have appropriate qualifications, level of access and defined responsibilities to carry out their assigned duties."
- **3.1 Suppliers and Service Providers** [PARTIAL] "When third parties are used … formal agreements must exist between the manufacturer and any third parties."
- **3.2** [PARTIAL] "The competence and reliability of a supplier are key factors when selecting a product or service provider." — *(the official clause continues that the need for an audit should be based on a risk assessment)*
- **3.3** [PARTIAL] "Documentation supplied with commercial off-the-shelf products should be reviewed by regulated users."
- **3.4** [PARTIAL] "Quality system and audit information relating to suppliers should be made available to inspectors on request."
- **4.1 Validation** [PARTIAL] "Manufacturers should be able to justify their standards, protocols, acceptance criteria, procedures and records."
- **4.2** [PARTIAL] "Validation documentation should include change control records and reports on any deviations observed."
- **4.3 System inventory** [PARTIAL] "An up to date listing of all relevant systems and their GMP functionality should be available."
- **4.4 URS** [PARTIAL] "User Requirements Specifications should describe the required functions of the computerised system." — *(official clause continues: based on documented risk assessment and GMP impact; requirements should be traceable throughout the life-cycle — this is the explicit EU basis for a traceability matrix)*
- **4.5** [PARTIAL] "The regulated user should take all reasonable steps to ensure system development followed appropriate quality management."
- **4.6 Bespoke systems** [PARTIAL] "There should be a process ensuring formal assessment and reporting of quality for all life-cycle stages." — *(this is the bespoke/customised-system clause)*
- **4.7 Testing** [PARTIAL] "Evidence of appropriate test methods and test scenarios should be demonstrated."
- **4.8 Data migration** [PARTIAL] "Validation should include checks that data are not altered in value and/or meaning during migration."
- **5 Data** [PARTIAL] "Computerised systems exchanging data electronically should include appropriate built-in checks for correct and secure entry."
- **6 Accuracy checks** [PARTIAL] "For critical data entered manually, there should be an additional check on the accuracy of the data."
- **7.1 Data storage** [PARTIAL] "Data should be secured by both physical and electronic means against damage."
- **7.2 Back-ups** [PARTIAL] "Regular back-ups of all relevant data should be done." — *(official clause continues: integrity and accuracy of backup data and the ability to restore the data should be checked during validation and monitored periodically — this is the explicit basis for backup/restore testing in OQ)*
- **8.1 Printouts** [PARTIAL] "It should be possible to obtain clear printed copies of electronically stored data."
- **8.2** [PARTIAL] "For records supporting batch release it should be possible to generate printouts indicating if any data changed."
- **9 Audit trails** [PARTIAL] "Consideration should be given … to building into the system the creation of a record of all GMP-relevant changes." — *(official clause continues: and deletions, a system generated "audit trail"; reason for change should be documented; audit trails need to be available, convertible to a generally intelligible form and regularly reviewed)*
- **10 Change and configuration management** [PARTIAL] "Any changes to a computerised system should only be made in a controlled manner in accordance with procedure."
- **11 Periodic evaluation** [PARTIAL] "Computerised systems should be periodically evaluated to confirm that they remain in a valid state." — *(official clause continues: and are compliant with GMP; evaluations should include, where appropriate, the current range of functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems, upgrade history, performance, reliability, security and validation status reports)*
- **12.1 Security** [PARTIAL] "Physical and/or logical controls should be in place to restrict access to computerised systems to authorised persons."
- **12.2** [PARTIAL] "The extent of security controls depends on the criticality of the computerised system."
- **12.3** [PARTIAL] "Creation, change, and cancellation of access authorisations should be recorded."
- **12.4** [PARTIAL] "Management systems … should be designed to record the identity of operators entering, changing or deleting data."
- **13 Incident management** [PARTIAL] "All incidents … should be reported and assessed."
- **14 Electronic signature** [PARTIAL] Electronic signatures "should be permanently linked to their respective record" and "include the time and date."
- **16 Business continuity** [PARTIAL] "Provisions should be made to ensure continuity of support for critical processes in event of system breakdown."
- **17 Archiving** [PARTIAL] "Data should be checked for accessibility, readability and integrity."

### Annex 11 revision status (relevant for a product sold from 2026 onwards)

- A **draft revision of Annex 11 was released 7 July 2025**, with a **consultation deadline of 7 October 2025** and **final publication expected in 2026**. — [ECA Academy / gmp-compliance.org](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- The draft adds a dedicated **Supplier Management section (Section 7)** with roughly nine subsections on audits, contracts and documentation for external IT companies. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- The draft adds a dedicated **Periodic Review section (Section 14)** with roughly twelve subsections, which "notably [was] absent from the 2011 version." — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- The draft adds **Audit Trail Management (Section 12)** covering technical setup and on-time review, and **IT Security (Section 15)** requiring firewalls, disaster recovery, patching, virus protection and mandatory **penetration tests for critical systems**. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- **Validation Approach (draft Section 9)**: systems may operate "in a limited scope even if validation has not been fully completed" if explicitly documented. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- Draft Sections 10–11 emphasise electronic interfaces over manual data entry, encryption, strict access control, and notably reject smart cards as a sole authentication factor. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- A related new **Annex 22 on Artificial Intelligence** is in progress and "explicitly aims to align with … GAMP 5." — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)

### Inferences
- The 2025 draft's new supplier-management and periodic-review sections are effectively a roadmap for what a small software supplier will be asked for from 2026/2027 onward: a written supplier quality agreement, documented audit rights, a release/change procedure, and an annual-review data pack. Building these now is a competitive differentiator.
- The penetration-testing expectation in draft Section 15 is a genuine new cost item for a bespoke web/cloud monitoring platform.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from a primary source whether the 2011 Annex 11 remains the version legally in force as of September 2026, or whether the revised version has since been adopted and with what transition period. **This must be re-checked at health.ec.europa.eu before the package is sold.** The ECA analysis (of the draft) only says final publication was "expected in 2026".
- Annex 11 clause 15 (batch release) was returned only in abbreviated form; clauses were generally truncated.

## Q3: What should a URS for a temperature monitoring system contain?

### Takeaway
The URS is mandated by Annex 15 clause 3.2 and Annex 11 clause 4.4, must be risk-assessed and traceable, and for a monitoring system must cover measurement performance, alarming/escalation, data integrity/audit trail, security, availability, and interfaces — each requirement individually numbered so it can be traced to a test.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 15 **3.1**: qualification "should consider all stages from initial development of the user requirements specification through to the end of use". — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **3.2**: "The specification for equipment, facilities, utilities or systems should be defined in a URS and/or a functional specification." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **4.4**: "User Requirements Specifications should describe the required functions of the computerised system." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- ELPRO's vendor V-model for temperature monitoring places, in order: **URS → Risk Analysis → Design Specification → IQ → OQ → PQ**, across four phases Plan / Build / Test / Use. — [ELPRO, Qualification & Mapping in Temperature Monitoring](https://www.elpro.com/en/temperature-monitoring/qualification-mapping)
- Eupry's warehouse qualification package is described as running from "URS checklists to IQ, OQ, and PQ protocol templates", aligned with WHO, FDA and GMP/GDP guidelines. — [Eupry, IQ OQ PQ for warehouses](https://eupry.com/iq-oq-pq/warehouses/)
- Mapping results should feed "recommendations for monitoring sensor placement and alarm limits" — i.e. the mapping report is an input to the monitoring-system URS/design, not an afterthought. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)

### Inferences (URS content checklist, derived from the clauses above)
A defensible URS for a LoRaWAN + bespoke-software temperature monitoring system should contain numbered, testable requirements in these groups:
1. **Scope & intended use** — which rooms/chambers/vehicles, which product storage conditions (2–8 °C, 15–25 °C, −20 °C), GMP vs GDP context, regulatory references.
2. **Measurement requirements** — measuring range, required accuracy/uncertainty (e.g. ±0.5 °C over 2–8 °C), resolution, sensor type, traceable calibration to a national standard, calibration interval, response time, logging interval (see finding below: 5–15 min is the cited practice).
3. **Sensor placement** — number and location of fixed sensors, justified by the temperature mapping report; worst-case (hot/cold spot) placement.
4. **Alarm requirements** — alarm limits (warning + action), delay/tolerance times, alarm on excursion, on sensor failure, on loss of communication, on low battery, on power loss; alarm acknowledgement and reason-for-acknowledgement recording.
5. **Notification & escalation** — channels (SMS/e-mail/app/voice), recipient lists, escalation ladders and timings, out-of-hours, confirmation of delivery.
6. **Data integrity / ALCOA+** — contemporaneous recording, no ability for any user to alter or delete raw measurement data, secure time stamping, audit trail of all GMP-relevant changes (Annex 11 §9), retention period, archiving and readability (§17).
7. **Security & access control** — unique user IDs, role model (read-only / operator / QA / admin), password policy, no shared accounts, recording of creation/change/cancellation of access rights (§12.3), session lock.
8. **Availability & continuity** — autonomous logging at the sensor/gateway when the network is down, buffer depth, automatic backfill, UPS/battery backup, backup frequency and restore capability (§7.2), business continuity in system breakdown (§16).
9. **Reporting** — periodic reports, excursion reports, printouts (§8.1), export formats, report content and signature.
10. **Time synchronisation** — single authoritative time source (NTP), time zone and DST handling, prohibition on user-settable device clocks.
11. **Interfaces** — LoRaWAN network server, integration to WMS/QMS if any, data export APIs.
12. **Regulatory/compliance requirements** — EU GMP Annex 11 and 15, EU GDP guidelines, GAMP 5, and (if applicable to the customer) 21 CFR Part 11.
13. **Non-functional** — number of sensors/users, performance, supported browsers, hosting model (on-prem vs cloud), supplier support and SLA.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative EU document prescribes a URS table of contents for monitoring systems specifically; the structure above is assembled from the clause requirements plus vendor practice, and should be presented as such.

## Q4: What exactly is tested in IQ for a temperature monitoring system?

### Takeaway
IQ = "everything is physically and logically there, correctly, and documented" — installation verified against drawings/specifications and against pre-defined criteria, instrument calibration, and collection of supplier documentation (Annex 15 §3.9).

### Cited Findings
- Annex 15 **3.9** mandates, as a minimum: verification of correct installation against engineering drawings and specifications; verification against pre-defined criteria; collection and collation of supplier operating/working instructions and maintenance requirements; calibration of instrumentation; verification of materials of construction. — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- For warehouses, IQ verifies facility readiness through "design documentation", "as-built drawings, HVAC schematics, airflow design, and insulation details", utility confirmation, "equipment identification" **with serial numbers**, "calibration evidence" for reference instruments and fixed probes, and **configuration baseline recording**. — [Eupry, IQ OQ PQ for warehouses](https://eupry.com/iq-oq-pq/warehouses/)
- For a temperature monitoring system specifically, IQ covers: **sensor positioning verification** (placement per the validation plan), **communication system testing** (verifying wireless device connections), **environmental condition documentation** of the installation area, **documentation review** of equipment certificates and calibration records, and **utility and backup system verification** (power connections and backup functionality). — [SenseAnywhere](https://www.senseanywhere.com/what-are-the-iq-oq-and-pq-phases-of-temperature-monitoring-system-qualification/)
- Annex 11 **4.3** requires an up-to-date listing of all relevant systems and their GMP functionality — i.e. the IQ inventory feeds the customer's system inventory. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)

### Inferences (concrete IQ test items for a LoRaWAN + server installation)
An IQ protocol for this system should contain these numbered test/verification items with evidence attached:
- **IQ-01 Hardware inventory**: every sensor/logger, gateway, antenna, server, UPS listed with manufacturer, model, **serial number**, asset tag, physical location.
- **IQ-02 Sensor location verification**: each sensor's location documented against the site plan/mapping report, with a photo and a location ID; confirmation that locations match those recommended by the mapping study.
- **IQ-03 Calibration certificates**: current, traceable calibration certificate for every sensor and for the reference standard, including calibration points, uncertainty, issuing lab accreditation, calibration date and due date.
- **IQ-04 Firmware/software versions**: recorded version of sensor firmware, gateway firmware, LoRaWAN network server, application software, OS, database — this is the *configuration baseline* against which future change control is judged.
- **IQ-05 Network configuration**: LoRaWAN frequency plan, DevEUI/AppEUI/keys management, gateway IP/connectivity, firewall rules, ports, TLS, network diagram.
- **IQ-06 Server specification**: hardware or VM specification, CPU/RAM/disk, OS and patch level, hosting location (on-prem / data centre / cloud region), storage and retention sizing, NTP source, antivirus, backup agent.
- **IQ-07 Installation verification**: physical mounting, cabling, power supply, UPS connection, sensor probe immersion/positioning, gateway coverage/RSSI check per sensor.
- **IQ-08 Documentation collation**: user manual, administrator manual, installation manual, maintenance instructions, SOP drafts, supplier release notes, software licence, supplier quality documentation.
- **IQ-09 Environment**: ambient conditions of server room / gateway location.
- **IQ-10 Account and role setup baseline**: list of created user accounts and assigned roles at hand-over.

### Gaps
- No EU regulatory text enumerates these ten items; they are the standard interpretation of Annex 15 §3.9 plus Annex 11 §4.3 combined with vendor practice. Present as best practice, not as quoted requirement.

## Q5: What exactly is tested in OQ?

### Takeaway
OQ = "the system does what it is supposed to do, including at its limits and in worst case" (Annex 15 §3.11). For a monitoring system, the worst cases are excursions, sensor failure, power failure and network failure — and OQ is where alarms, data integrity, audit trail, access control, backup/restore and time synchronisation are challenged.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 15 **3.11**: OQ must include "tests that have been developed from the knowledge of processes, systems and equipment to ensure the system is operating as designed" and "tests to confirm upper and lower operating limits, and /or 'worst case' conditions." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **3.12**: successful OQ "should allow the finalisation of standard operating and cleaning procedures, operator training and preventative maintenance requirements" — i.e. SOPs and training are OQ outputs. — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Warehouse OQ tests controls and alarms via: "functional checks" of HVAC response and sensor interfaces; **"alarm challenges" simulating temperature excursions and measuring response times**; "door cycle tests" assessing dock infiltration and recovery; "stress mapping" with varied loads to identify worst-case locations; and **"data integrity checks" for time sync and audit trails**. — [Eupry, IQ OQ PQ for warehouses](https://eupry.com/iq-oq-pq/warehouses/)
- Monitoring-system OQ specifically includes: **calibration verification** — "testing sensors at multiple temperature points within your operating range"; **alarm functionality testing** — confirming high/low alerts trigger correctly; **data integrity validation** — ensuring accurate recording and storage without corruption; **system stability testing** — verifying performance during network interruptions and power fluctuations; **security feature verification** — testing user access controls. — [SenseAnywhere](https://www.senseanywhere.com/what-are-the-iq-oq-and-pq-phases-of-temperature-monitoring-system-qualification/)
- Annex 11 **7.2** requires that the ability to restore backed-up data be checked during validation and monitored periodically — the regulatory basis for a backup/restore OQ test. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **9** requires a system-generated audit trail of GMP-relevant changes and deletions, available and convertible to an intelligible form and regularly reviewed — the basis for an audit-trail OQ test. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **12.1–12.4** require logical access restriction, recording of creation/change/cancellation of access authorisations, and recording of operator identity — the basis for a user-access/roles OQ test. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **8.1** requires that clear printed copies of electronically stored data can be obtained — the basis for a report/printout OQ test. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **16** requires provisions for continuity of support for critical processes in the event of system breakdown — the basis for power/network failure OQ tests. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Recording interval practice: "temperatures should be recorded every 5–15 minutes to detect fluctuations". — [ELPRO](https://www.elpro.com/en/temperature-monitoring/qualification-mapping)

### Inferences (how to actually provoke each OQ test — concrete method)

**A. Sensor accuracy verification against a reference standard**
- Place the fixed sensor and a calibrated reference thermometer (traceable, with valid certificate, uncertainty smaller than the tolerance being demonstrated — typically at least 4:1 TUR) in a stable bath/calibrator or a stable chamber.
- Test at **multiple points across the operating range** (e.g. for a 2–8 °C application: 0 °C, 5 °C, 10 °C; for a 15–25 °C application: 15 °C, 20 °C, 25 °C; for freezers: −25 °C, −20 °C, −15 °C).
- Acceptance criterion: deviation from reference within the URS-stated accuracy (typically ±0.5 °C), recorded for every sensor.
- Evidence: reference certificate, raw readings, deviation table, pass/fail per sensor.

**B. Alarm function testing — how to provoke an alarm (four methods, in order of preference)**
1. **Physical stimulus (preferred, most defensible):** remove the sensor from the controlled space, or immerse it in a warm/ice bath, or place it in a calibrated dry-block set above/below the alarm limit, and let the real measured value cross the real alarm limit. Records the *full chain*: measurement → limit evaluation → alarm state → notification.
2. **Temporary limit change (second best):** temporarily narrow the alarm limits (e.g. set the high limit to just below current ambient) so the real measured value breaches them; document the limit change in the audit trail, then restore. Useful for freezers where physical stimulus is slow.
3. **Door-open / power-off challenge:** open the chamber door or switch off refrigeration until the temperature crosses the limit — also demonstrates alarm delay and recovery.
4. **Simulated/injected value (weakest; only acceptable where justified and documented):** use a test/simulation mode. Inspectors are sceptical because it bypasses the sensing chain.
- For each test record: time of excursion onset, alarm limit, alarm delay setting, time alarm raised, time notification sent, time notification received by each recipient, escalation steps, acknowledgement, and the record in the audit trail.

**C. Limit testing** — verify high limit, low limit, and (if used) warning vs action limits separately; verify the alarm delay/tolerance time (alarm must NOT fire for an excursion shorter than the configured delay, and MUST fire once it exceeds it — test both sides); verify alarm hysteresis/return-to-normal.

**D. Notification / escalation testing** — for each configured channel (e-mail, SMS, push, voice) and each recipient, verify delivery and content; verify the escalation ladder by *not* acknowledging the first alarm and confirming escalation to the second and third recipient after the configured intervals; test out-of-hours/on-call rota if configured; verify that an unreachable recipient triggers fallback.

**E. Power failure testing** — disconnect mains from (i) a sensor/logger, (ii) the gateway, (iii) the server/UPS. Verify: local buffering continues, a power-loss alarm is raised, data are not lost, after restoration data are automatically backfilled with correct original timestamps and are flagged appropriately, and the system returns to normal without manual reconstruction.

**F. Network failure testing** — disconnect the gateway from the internet / block the LoRaWAN uplink for a defined period (e.g. 1–4 h). Verify: the sensor continues logging autonomously; a communication-loss alarm is raised after the configured timeout; on reconnection all buffered readings are transferred with their *original* timestamps; no duplicates, no gaps; buffer capacity is sufficient for the URS-stated autonomy period.

**G. Sensor failure / disconnection testing** — physically disconnect or remove a probe; verify a sensor-fault alarm rather than a silently frozen last value. Also test low-battery alarm where applicable.

**H. User access and roles testing** — for each role: attempt permitted and forbidden actions and document the result (e.g. read-only user cannot change alarm limits; operator cannot delete data; only admin can create users). Test: login with valid/invalid password, account lockout after n failed attempts, password complexity and expiry, session timeout, prevention of duplicate/shared user IDs, deactivation of a user immediately removing access. Verify that creation, change and cancellation of access rights are recorded (Annex 11 §12.3).

**I. Audit trail testing** — perform a set of deliberate GMP-relevant actions (change an alarm limit, acknowledge an alarm with a reason, create/deactivate a user, change a sensor configuration, attempt a data deletion) and verify each appears in the audit trail with: who, what (old value → new value), when (with time zone), and why (reason). Verify the audit trail is **not editable or deletable by any user including admin**, is human-readable/exportable, and can be filtered and printed.

**J. Data integrity testing** — verify raw measurement data cannot be altered or deleted from the application or (where relevant) by direct database access controlled by procedure; verify that an exported report matches the source data; verify checksum/record protection if implemented; verify that a record survives a forced interruption mid-write; verify retention of data for the required period.

**K. Backup and restore testing** — trigger a backup, verify it completes and is stored per specification; then **restore to a test instance and prove the restored data are complete, accurate and readable**, including audit trail. This is explicitly demanded by Annex 11 §7.2. Record RPO/RTO achieved.

**L. Report generation testing** — generate each report type (periodic report, excursion report, calibration status, audit-trail report, alarm history); verify content correctness against raw data, correct time zone, correct headers (system, period, sensor IDs, limits), pagination, "end of report" marker, and that a clear printout/PDF can be produced (Annex 11 §8.1).

**M. Time synchronisation testing** — verify all components (sensor/logger, gateway, network server, application server, database) take time from a single authoritative source (NTP); verify time zone and DST handling (a record made at the DST change must be unambiguous); verify a user cannot alter the system clock or a record's timestamp; verify that the timestamp on a buffered reading after a network outage is the *time of measurement*, not the time of upload.

**N. Calculation/derived value testing** — where the software computes MKT (mean kinetic temperature), averages, min/max or excursion duration, verify against an independent hand calculation.

**O. Configuration/limit change control testing** — verify that changing an alarm limit requires the correct privilege, is recorded in the audit trail, and takes effect as documented.

### Gaps
- I found no EU regulatory or standard text that enumerates alarm-provocation methods; items B–O above are an engineering synthesis from the clause requirements plus the vendor sources cited. They should be presented to a customer as the supplier's method, justified by clause references, not as a quotation.
- I did not find a published, inspected example OQ protocol for a LoRaWAN monitoring system with named test-case IDs.

## Q6: What exactly is tested in PQ, and how long should PQ runs be?

### Takeaway
PQ = the system performing under real, routine, loaded conditions over an extended period. Annex 15 §3.14 requires tests under normal operating conditions with worst case, covering the operating range. Published durations for monitoring systems cluster around **4–8 weeks for PQ**, and for the associated temperature mapping around **7–14 days**, with seasonal (summer/winter) repetition where risk-justified.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 15 **3.14**: PQ tests use "production materials, qualified substitutes or simulated product proven to have equivalent behaviour under normal operating conditions with worst case batch sizes" and "should cover the operating range of the intended process". — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **3.13**: "PQ should normally follow the successful completion of IQ and OQ. However, it may in some cases be appropriate to perform it in conjunction with OQ or Process Validation." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Warehouse PQ demonstrates routine performance through "mapping under representative load" with typical pallet patterns, inclusion of "peak activity windows, door cycles, and material flows", and "seasonal confirmation" at climatic extremes when risk-justified. — [Eupry, IQ OQ PQ for warehouses](https://eupry.com/iq-oq-pq/warehouses/)
- Monitoring-system PQ includes: long-term stability studies across varying environmental conditions; **sensor drift monitoring**; communication reliability assessment (wireless connectivity consistency); worst-case scenario testing (power failures and network outages); and data integrity verification during extended operation. — [SenseAnywhere](https://www.senseanywhere.com/what-are-the-iq-oq-and-pq-phases-of-temperature-monitoring-system-qualification/)
- **Duration:** the complete IQ/OQ/PQ process "typically requires 4–12 weeks, with PQ specifically requiring 4–8 weeks of extended monitoring to demonstrate consistent real-world performance." — [SenseAnywhere](https://www.senseanywhere.com/what-are-the-iq-oq-and-pq-phases-of-temperature-monitoring-system-qualification/)
- **Mapping duration:** "7–14 days is typical, but the duration must be justified by risk"; regulators expect justification if seasonal studies are not conducted. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)
- **Mapping sensor counts:** "a smaller area may be covered by as little as 15 data loggers, while larger warehouses can require more than 300", placed at likely hot/cold spots, corners, door areas and representative zones on a risk basis rather than a fixed grid. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)
- **Chamber/fridge sensor placement:** "at least one probe is placed in each corner, plus the center, and near potential problem areas", the number depending on size and complexity, risk-based, aligned with USP and WHO guidance; loggers are placed "for several days or weeks". — [ELPRO](https://www.elpro.com/en/temperature-monitoring/qualification-mapping)
- **Remapping / requalification frequency:** mapping should be performed initially on installation, "every 1–3 years depending on risk", after significant changes (relocation, repairs, layout modification, equipment malfunction), and seasonally where required for summer/winter extremes. — [ELPRO](https://www.elpro.com/en/temperature-monitoring/qualification-mapping)
- Alternatively, requalification is triggered "when changes in layout, HVAC, storage capacity, or if trend data indicates instability", or continuous-mapping frameworks can be adopted to avoid discrete remapping campaigns. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)
- The mapping report should demonstrate protocol adherence, present temperature profiles and excursions, state acceptance decisions with rationale, and give **recommendations for monitoring sensor placement and alarm limits**. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)

### Inferences (PQ test content for a monitoring system)
- **PQ-01 Extended continuous operation** over the agreed period (commonly 30 days; 4–8 weeks per the cited vendor guidance) with the warehouse in routine use — full load, normal door traffic, normal goods movements.
- **PQ-02 Data completeness metric**: percentage of expected readings actually received and stored per sensor over the run (e.g. ≥99.5 % acceptance criterion) — this is the single most convincing PQ metric for a wireless system.
- **PQ-03 Communication reliability**: number and duration of transmission gaps per sensor; RSSI/SNR trend; gateway uptime.
- **PQ-04 Alarm performance in real operation**: every real alarm in the period reviewed — was it genuine, was it delivered, was it acknowledged in time, was the escalation correct; false-alarm rate.
- **PQ-05 Comparison against an independent reference/mapping logger** placed alongside a fixed sensor for the PQ period, demonstrating agreement within tolerance over time (also evidences drift).
- **PQ-06 Sensor drift check** — post-PQ verification/calibration check against the reference showing drift within limits.
- **PQ-07 Battery performance** and projected battery life for the LoRaWAN nodes.
- **PQ-08 Server/application stability** — uptime, backup success rate, error logs reviewed, no unexplained gaps.
- **PQ-09 Data integrity over the run** — audit trail reviewed for the period; no unexplained modifications.
- **PQ-10 Seasonal worst case** — either run PQ in the hottest/coldest season, or run a second short confirmation run in the opposite season, or justify by risk assessment using mapping data.
- A defensible commercial default: **30 consecutive days PQ minimum**, with seasonal confirmation, and PQ deliberately including at least one deliberate worst-case event (a planned power cut and a planned network cut) if these were not already covered in OQ.

### Gaps
- There is **no EU regulatory text that specifies a PQ duration** for monitoring systems. The 4–8 week figure comes from a vendor (SenseAnywhere) and the 7–14 day mapping figure from another vendor (Eupry); these are industry practice, not law. Annex 15 requires only that the approach be justified. This distinction must be stated honestly to customers.
- No primary EU source was found prescribing a specific minimum number of fixed monitoring sensors per m³ or per chamber.

## Q7: Role of GAMP 5 (2nd edition, 2022) — categories, bespoke software, supplier leverage

### Takeaway
GAMP 5 is not law but is the de-facto expected methodology in the EU; its software categories set the validation effort. **Bespoke monitoring/SCADA software is unambiguously Category 5**, which means a full documented software development lifecycle — the heaviest category — and it also means the customer will scrutinise the supplier, because for Category 5 there is no vendor track record to lean on.

### Cited Findings — categories
- **Category 1 — Infrastructure software**: operating systems, databases, virtualisation platforms used as supplied; examples Windows Server, Red Hat Linux, Oracle DB, MS SQL Server, VMware vSphere, Active Directory. Deliverables: "configuration documentation + change control only"; "validation typically requires only configuration records and installation verification." — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/); [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- **Category 3 — Non-configured products**: commercial software used exactly as supplied, no custom workflows or fields; examples Excel used for read-only reference data, standard PDF readers, fixed-function lab instrument firmware, network monitoring tools. Deliverables: "functional testing for intended use"; installation verification plus functional testing of GxP-relevant features. — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/); [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- **Category 4 — Configured products**: "the most common category in pharma"; examples SAP S/4HANA, Veeva Vault QMS, LabWare LIMS, MasterControl. "You validate your configuration, not the base platform." Deliverables: "full IQ/OQ/PQ on your configuration" with an RTM linking requirements to test cases. Category 4 "allows leveraging vendor evidence." — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/)
- **Category 5 — Custom software**: "built specifically for you — including any custom script that processes GxP data, regardless of code length"; examples explicitly include **bespoke SCADA/DCS systems**, custom MES/lab automation, Python/R/VBA scripts on GxP data, and custom integrations. Deliverables: "full SDLC lifecycle validation" with complete documentation rigour and "full testing rigour and unscripted documentation." — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/)
- **Custom environmental monitoring or SCADA systems are explicitly stated to be Category 5** because they are "built specifically for your organisation." — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/)
- Category 5 "demands full development life cycle documentation and code reviews plus rigorous testing across unit through PQ phases." — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- Effort scales with category: "the more novel or custom a system is, the more validation work is expected." — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)
- Category 4 vs 5 in supplier terms: Category 4 (standard, configurable) requires configuration only, and design and code testing documentation suffice if the vendor provides adequate evidence; Category 5 (custom/programmed) requires a full lifecycle model combining system development and integration, "necessitating substantially more rigorous documentation and testing." — [Osborne, "GAMP 5 and the supplier", Performance PharmaTech](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)

### Cited Findings — V-model, traceability, supplier leverage
- The V-model maps development activities (left: URS → FS → DS) to corresponding qualifications (right: PQ, OQ, IQ) and "ensures traceability between specifications and test results", critical for higher-category systems and regulatory defence. — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- "A traceability matrix is usually created to map each requirement through the design and testing process, ensuring nothing is missed." The V-model inherently demonstrates traceability, establishing bidirectional mapping between specifications and test evidence. — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)
- "Regulated Pharmaceutical companies should seek to maximise supplier involvement throughout the system life cycle", subject to satisfactory assessment; supplier documentation should be evaluated for "suitability, accuracy and completeness", with flexibility in format and structure. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- Organisations should "leverage supplier testing documentation and quality management records" to eliminate duplicative effort while maintaining compliance. — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- **Critical limit on leverage:** "the supplier is not responsible for the testing, the end customer is" and the customer will report during regulatory audits. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- **Documentation package a software supplier should provide to a regulated customer**: application-level functional specifications; design specifications; design review / DQ details; software configuration documentation; testing references; user documentation; training details; CE marking documentation; release and change management procedures; and audit results from qualified independent auditors. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- GAMP 5 Second Edition (2022) emphasises: professional judgment / critical thinking in proportional risk assessment; cloud and SaaS validation guidance; ALCOA++ data integrity; cybersecurity integrated into validation; CSA alignment; and agile/iterative development, recognising that "modern development does not always follow a linear path." — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)

### Inferences
- Because the sole trader writes his own software, the deliverable set is the **Category 5 set**, which in practice means: URS → Functional Specification → Design/Software Design Specification → configuration specification → source-code management and code review records → unit/integration test records → **Requirements Traceability Matrix** → IQ/OQ/PQ → Validation Summary Report, plus an SDLC procedure, a change/release procedure, and a defect/incident procedure.
- There is a commercially meaningful escape hatch: if the same software is sold as a **standard product to many customers**, and the customer only configures (alarm limits, users, sensor names, report templates) without any customer-specific code, it can be argued and documented as **Category 4** at the customer site, with the Category 5 rigour absorbed once by the supplier in his own internal product lifecycle. The customer-facing package then becomes "validate the configuration", which is a much cheaper and more repeatable sale. This argument must be written down in the validation plan / risk assessment and supported by evidence that no customer-specific code exists. GAMP 5 categorisation is about how the software is produced and deployed, so this should be documented deliberately rather than assumed.
- Category 5 is also why the supplier will be audited: with no installed base of identical validated instances, the customer's only assurance is the supplier's own quality system.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the ISPE GAMP 5 2nd edition primary text (paywalled, ISPE members/purchase only, https://ispe.org). All category descriptions above are from secondary commentary; exact GAMP wording and the appendix numbering (e.g. Appendix M4 categories, Appendix M2 supplier assessment) could not be verified.
- GAMP 5 2nd edition removed Category 2 (firmware) — this is widely reported but I did not confirm it from a primary source in this research.

## Q8: Change control when the supplier releases a new software version at a validated customer site

### Takeaway
A software release at a validated site is a **change subject to the customer's change control system (Annex 11 §10, Annex 15 §11)**; the supplier's obligation is to give the customer everything needed to perform a risk assessment and decide the regression scope. The regulatory trigger sentence is Annex 15 clause 11.4.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 11 **10**: "Any changes to a computerised system should only be made in a controlled manner in accordance with procedure." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **11.2** requires written procedures describing actions to be taken for a planned change to "equipment … or any other change during the lifecycle that may affect product quality or reproducibility." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **11.4** requires quality risk management to evaluate planned changes "to determine the potential impact on product quality, pharmaceutical quality systems, documentation, validation, regulatory status, calibration, maintenance and on any other system to avoid unintended consequences **and to plan for any necessary process validation, verification or requalification efforts**." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **11.5**: "Changes should be authorised and approved by the responsible persons or relevant functional personnel in accordance with the pharmaceutical quality system." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **4.2**: "Validation documentation should include change control records and reports on any deviations observed." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **11**: systems "should be periodically evaluated to confirm that they remain in a valid state", with the evaluation including (per the official clause) the current range of functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems, **upgrade history**, performance, reliability, security and validation status reports. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- A software supplier is expected to provide, among other things, "release/change management procedures" as part of its documentation package to regulated customers. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- The 2025 Annex 11 draft adds a dedicated Supplier Management section (Section 7, ~9 subsections) covering audits, contracts and documentation for external IT companies, tightening this expectation. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)

### Inferences (what the supplier must actually ship with every release)
A release pack for a validated customer site should contain:
1. **Release notes** — new version number, previous version number, release date, and an itemised list of changes split into: new functions, changed functions, bug fixes, security patches, and changes to the database schema.
2. **Change classification / impact statement per change** — GxP-relevant yes/no, which URS requirement(s) it touches, which validated function(s) it touches. This is what lets the customer's QA do the Annex 15 §11.4 risk assessment without reverse-engineering the code.
3. **Supplier-side test evidence** — unit/integration/system test report for the release, executed against the supplier's own test instance, with pass/fail and any known open defects.
4. **Recommended regression test scope at the customer site** — the supplier's proposal of which OQ test cases to re-execute, with rationale for what is *not* re-executed (this rationale is the document inspectors ask for).
5. **Updated documentation** — updated URS/FS/design spec where functions changed, updated user manual, updated traceability matrix.
6. **Installation/upgrade instructions and rollback plan** — including database migration steps and, per Annex 11 §4.8, evidence that migrated data are not altered in value or meaning.
7. **Known issues / defect list** carried into the release.
8. **Security statement** — CVEs addressed, dependency updates.
9. **Statement of validation status** — supplier's declaration of what has been verified and what remains the customer's responsibility.

**Regression test scope — what it should cover (risk-based, per Annex 15 §11.4):**
- Every OQ test case whose function was directly changed (mandatory).
- Every OQ test case for a function that shares code, data or configuration with a changed function (integration risk).
- The **core critical-path tests re-run for every release regardless of change content**, because they are what patient safety depends on: measurement acquisition and storage; alarm limit evaluation and alarm raising; notification delivery; audit trail recording and immutability; user access control; data export/report correctness; time synchronisation and timestamp correctness; data continuity through network loss.
- Post-upgrade data integrity check: records created before the upgrade are still complete, readable and unchanged; audit trail history preserved; report over a period spanning the upgrade is correct.
- Backup/restore verified after the upgrade.
- The result is documented as a **regression test report** referenced by the customer's change control record; the change control is closed only after review of effectiveness (Annex 15 §11.x on post-implementation evaluation).

**Practical model for the sole trader:** maintain a standing "regression test set" (say 15–25 test cases drawn from the OQ) and sell an annual/per-release "re-qualification on upgrade" service where those cases are re-executed and reported. This turns change control into recurring revenue and is exactly what customers need.

### Gaps
- I could not locate an EU regulatory text that defines "regression testing" or prescribes its scope; the scope above is derived from the risk-assessment obligation in Annex 15 §11.4 and from GAMP practice, not quoted from a guideline.
- Annex 15 clause 11.6 (post-implementation evaluation of change effectiveness) was not returned by the fetch; Section 11 may continue past 11.5 and should be checked.

## Q9: Periodic review / requalification — frequency and scope

### Takeaway
Two separate obligations apply: **Annex 11 §11 periodic evaluation of the computerised system** and **Annex 15 §4.1–4.2 re-qualification of equipment/systems at a justified frequency**. Neither states a number — the interval must be risk-justified by the customer, and industry practice for critical GxP systems commonly lands at 1–3 years.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 11 **11**: "Computerised systems should be periodically evaluated to confirm that they remain in a valid state" (and are compliant with GMP). The official clause specifies the evaluation should include, where appropriate: the current range of functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems, upgrade history, performance, reliability, security and validation status reports. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **4.1**: "Equipment, facilities, utilities and systems should be evaluated at an appropriate frequency to confirm that they remain in a state of control." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **4.2**: "Where re-qualification is necessary and performed at a specific time period, the period should be justified and the criteria for evaluation defined." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- Annex 15 **1.5 vii**: the VMP must include "the qualification and validation strategy, including requalification, where applicable." — [Annex 15, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/2015-10_annex15_0.pdf)
- The 2025 Annex 11 draft introduces a **dedicated Periodic Review section (Section 14) with about twelve subsections**, which did not exist in the 2011 version — a strong signal that inspection focus on periodic review will increase. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)
- For the physical/mapping side: temperature mapping should be repeated "every 1–3 years depending on risk", and after relocation, repairs, layout modifications or equipment malfunction; seasonal repetition is sometimes required. — [ELPRO](https://www.elpro.com/en/temperature-monitoring/qualification-mapping)
- Alternatively, remapping is triggered by changes in layout, HVAC or storage capacity, or when trend data indicate instability. — [Eupry, warehouse mapping guide](https://eupry.com/temperature-mapping/warehouse-mapping-guide/)
- Sensor calibration is required to be traceable for both reference instruments and fixed probes. — [Eupry, IQ OQ PQ for warehouses](https://eupry.com/iq-oq-pq/warehouses/)

### Inferences (periodic review scope — what the report should contain)
A periodic review report for a monitoring system (annual for a critical system; the interval justified in a risk assessment) should cover:
1. System description and current configuration vs the validated baseline (hardware, firmware, software versions, sensor list and locations).
2. **Validation status** — which IQ/OQ/PQ documents are current, any gaps.
3. **Change history** since the last review — every change/upgrade, its change control reference, and whether the regression testing was adequate.
4. **Deviations, incidents and alarms** — list, root causes, CAPAs, open items; alarm statistics and false-alarm rate.
5. **Audit trail review** — evidence that periodic audit-trail review has been performed, and its findings.
6. **User access review** — current user list vs HR/authorisation list; leavers removed; privilege creep; shared accounts.
7. **Calibration status** — every sensor's calibration in date; drift trend; any out-of-tolerance findings and their retrospective data impact assessment.
8. **Backup and restore** — backup success record for the period, and evidence that a restore test has been performed.
9. **Security** — patch level, vulnerabilities, security incidents, penetration test status (anticipating draft Annex 11 §15).
10. **Performance/availability** — uptime, data completeness percentage, communication gaps.
11. **SOPs and training** — are procedures current, are users trained on the current version.
12. **Supplier status** — supplier still in business and supported, contract/quality agreement current, supplier audit due, version still supported (not end-of-life).
13. **Conclusion** — system remains in a validated state / requires action; whether requalification (partial or full) is triggered; actions with owners and dates.

**Requalification triggers** to write into the customer's SOP: major software version upgrade; server migration or change of hosting; change of sensor type or firmware; relocation or change of monitored area; changes to the warehouse layout/HVAC affecting hot/cold spots; repeated unexplained excursions or data gaps; out-of-tolerance calibration; findings from periodic review; expiry of the justified requalification interval.

### Gaps
- **No primary source was found that states a specific mandatory periodic-review interval in years.** Common industry practice of annual review for high-criticality and 2–3 years for lower-criticality systems is widely repeated in secondary commentary, but I could not verify it against a regulatory or standards source in this research. This should be presented to customers as "the interval must be justified", not as "every N years is required".

## Q10: Supplier audit / supplier questionnaire — what pharma customers ask a small software supplier

### Takeaway
Annex 11 §3.2 makes supplier competence and reliability the basis of selection, with the need for an audit determined by risk — so a small Category 5 supplier will routinely receive a **supplier quality questionnaire**, and for a bespoke system may receive a full **supplier audit** (remote or on-site). Preparing a standing "supplier qualification dossier" pre-empts most of this.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 11 **3.1**: formal agreements must exist between the manufacturer and any third parties. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **3.2**: "The competence and reliability of a supplier are key factors when selecting a product or service provider." (Official clause continues that the need for an audit should be based on a risk assessment.) — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **3.3**: documentation supplied with commercial off-the-shelf products should be reviewed by regulated users. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **3.4**: "Quality system and audit information relating to suppliers should be made available to inspectors on request." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **4.5**: the regulated user should take all reasonable steps to ensure system development followed an appropriate quality management system. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Annex 11 **4.6**: for bespoke/customised systems there should be a process ensuring formal assessment and reporting of quality for all life-cycle stages. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- Expected supplier documentation package: application-level functional specifications, design specifications, design review/DQ details, software configuration documentation, testing references, user documentation, training details, CE marking documentation, release/change management procedures, and audit results from qualified independent auditors. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- "The supplier is not responsible for the testing, the end customer is" — the customer must own the validation even when the supplier writes it. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- Supplier involvement should be maximised throughout the lifecycle "subject to satisfactory assessment", and supplier documentation evaluated for "suitability, accuracy and completeness" with flexible format. — [Osborne, GAMP 5 and the supplier](https://secure.toolkitfiles.co.uk/clients/20735/sitedata/files/GAMP_5_and_the_supplier.pdf)
- Published supplier assessment questionnaire templates exist and are used industry-wide — e.g. the **Rx-360 Supplier Assessment Questionnaire** — [Rx-360](https://rx-360.org/supplier-assessment-questionnaire/) — and the **ECA Academy "Appendix 4: Supplier Quality Questionnaire — Extended Quality Questionnaire"** — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/guidelines/gmp-guideline/appendix-4-supplier-quality-questionnaire-instructions-for-completion-e-extended-quality-questionnaire) (content not retrievable — see Gaps).
- The 2025 Annex 11 draft adds a full Supplier Management section (Section 7) with ~nine subsections on audits, contracts and documentation for external IT companies. — [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/annex-11-draft-first-analysis)

### Inferences (typical question set a small software supplier will face)
Based on the Annex 11 §3–4 requirements and the documentation list above, the questionnaire/audit will typically cover:
- **Company & continuity:** legal entity, years in business, number of employees, key-person dependency, financial stability, insurance, escrow arrangement for source code, what happens if the supplier ceases trading.
- **Quality system:** is there a documented QMS; ISO 9001 / ISO 27001 / ISO 13485 certification; quality manual; list of SOPs; who is responsible for quality; internal audits; management review.
- **Regulatory awareness:** familiarity with EU GMP Annex 11 and 15, GAMP 5, 21 CFR Part 11, data integrity/ALCOA+; previous regulatory inspections at customer sites involving your product.
- **Software development lifecycle:** documented SDLC procedure; requirements management; design documentation; coding standards; code review; version control system; branching/release strategy; build reproducibility; use of third-party/open-source components and how they are tracked.
- **Testing:** test strategy; unit/integration/system test evidence; test environments; who tests; defect management system; how known defects are communicated to customers; regression test suite; automated testing.
- **Release and change management:** versioning scheme; release approval; release notes; upgrade/rollback; how customers are notified; support for older versions; end-of-life policy.
- **Data integrity and technical controls:** audit trail design; is raw data immutable; time source; user/role model; password policy; encryption at rest and in transit; database access controls; backup and restore; data retention and archiving; data export on contract termination.
- **Hosting/cloud (if SaaS):** data centre location (EU?), sub-processors, GDPR/DPA, uptime SLA, disaster recovery, RPO/RTO, penetration testing, vulnerability management, incident response.
- **Personnel:** CVs/training records of developers and validation staff; training on GMP/data integrity; job descriptions.
- **Subcontractors:** who else touches the code or the data; how they are qualified.
- **Documentation provision:** what validation documentation you supply; can the customer review your internal development documentation; do you permit an on-site or remote audit; do you accept audit rights in the contract.
- **Support:** SLA, response times, support channels, escalation, remote access controls (how supplier remote access is authorised, logged and restricted — a frequent inspection finding).

**Commercial recommendation:** produce a standing, pre-approved **Supplier Qualification Dossier** (company profile, QMS summary, SDLC procedure, change/release procedure, security statement, sample release notes, validation documentation index, and a pre-filled generic questionnaire response) so that each new customer's questionnaire takes hours, not weeks. Also prepare a template **Quality/Technical Agreement** (Annex 11 §3.1 requires a formal agreement) and a **source-code escrow** offer, which materially de-risks the "one-person supplier" objection.

If a real example is needed, the ECA Academy appendix and the Rx-360 template are the two concrete leads to chase.

## Q11: Traceability matrix — what it means here and is it required?

### Takeaway
A Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) links every URS requirement → specification/design element → test case → test result. It is not named as such in Annex 15, but Annex 11 §4.4 requires requirements to be traceable throughout the life-cycle, and GAMP 5's V-model builds traceability in — so in practice it is expected and inspectors ask for it.

### Cited Findings
- Annex 11 **4.4** requires the URS to describe required functions; the official clause continues that requirements should be traceable throughout the life-cycle. — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)
- "A traceability matrix is usually created to map each requirement through the design and testing process, ensuring nothing is missed." — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)
- The V-model "ensures traceability between specifications and test results", critical for higher-category systems and regulatory defence. — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- Category 4 deliverables explicitly include "an RTM linking requirements to test cases". — [GoValidation](https://govalidation.com/blog/gamp5-software-category-examples/)
- Traceability matrices appear in the standard CSV document set alongside validation plans, URS, test protocols (IQ/OQ/PQ), risk assessments and change control records. — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)

### Inferences
- Practical RTM format for this system: one row per URS requirement, with columns: URS ID | Requirement text | GMP-critical (Y/N) | Risk class | Functional/design spec reference | Test protocol and test case ID (IQ-xx / OQ-xx / PQ-xx) | Test result (pass/fail, date) | Deviation reference if any.
- The RTM is also the tool that makes change control cheap: when a release changes a function, the RTM instantly shows which test cases must be re-executed. This is worth building as a product feature, not just a Word table.
- Because the software is bespoke (Cat 5), the RTM should also trace down to design/code modules, not only to test cases.

### Gaps
- No EU clause uses the phrase "traceability matrix"; the requirement is derived from Annex 11 §4.4's traceability wording. Do not claim it is "required by Annex 15".

## Q12: CSV vs CSA — is FDA's CSA thinking relevant in the EU?

### Takeaway
CSA is a US FDA concept aimed at production and quality-system software; it is not EU law and there is no EU CSA guidance. However, its underlying logic (risk-proportionate effort, critical thinking, unscripted testing, use of system-generated evidence) was absorbed into **GAMP 5 Second Edition (2022)**, which is the framework EU inspectors expect — so CSA thinking is usable in the EU **only when expressed in GAMP 5 language and backed by a documented risk assessment**.

### Cited Findings
- GAMP 5 Second Edition includes "CSA alignment, bringing GAMP 5 closer to FDA's risk-based assurance approach"; "both GAMP 5 and CSA direct validation effort toward actual risk, calibrating testing depth to the compliance impact of each system." — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- CSA emphasises risk-based validation over documentation-heavy approaches; endorses "scripted testing, unscripted/exploratory testing, continuous monitoring"; prefers "digital-first documentation" (system logs, audit trails) over paper records; and provides "flexible testing methods" rather than rigid protocols. A **September 2025 FDA CSA guidance** is referenced. CSA "complements GAMP 5's principles" and represents FDA's "official endorsement of the risk-based, critical-thinking approach that GAMP has long advocated." CSA is positioned as guidance for **production and quality system software**, not pharmaceuticals broadly. — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)
- "No direct CSA/EU equivalence is discussed"; the EU route is the **draft Annex 11 revision (2025)** and the new **Annex 22 on AI**, which "explicitly aims to align with … GAMP 5", meaning GAMP remains the primary EU pharmaceutical validation framework rather than CSA. — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gamp-5-computerized-system-validation-pharma)
- GAMP 5 2nd edition also adds guidance on cloud/SaaS validation, ALCOA++, cybersecurity, and agile/iterative development. — [Kneat](https://kneat.com/article/what-is-gamp-5/)
- Annex 11 **4.1** already permits a justification-based approach: "Manufacturers should be able to justify their standards, protocols, acceptance criteria, procedures and records." — [Annex 11, EC](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical position for the Slovak supplier selling into EU pharma: **market and document the package as GMP Annex 11 / Annex 15 / GAMP 5 compliant**, not as "CSA". Use CSA-flavoured efficiencies (unscripted/exploratory testing for low-risk functions, screenshots and system logs as evidence, leveraging supplier test evidence) but always anchor them to a documented risk assessment and to Annex 11 §4.1's "should be able to justify" wording. A Slovak inspector (ŠÚKL) or a customer's QA will recognise Annex 11/15 and GAMP 5; "CSA" will invite questions.
- Where a customer also exports to the US or is FDA-inspected, mention CSA and 21 CFR Part 11 as an add-on, not as the primary basis.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the existence or exact title/date of a **September 2025 FDA CSA guidance** from an FDA primary source; the FDA CSA draft guidance was originally issued in September 2022 ("Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software"), and the secondary source's reference to a September 2025 document could be a finalisation or could be an error. **This must be checked on fda.gov before being quoted to a customer.**
- No EU regulator statement on CSA was found.
